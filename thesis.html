High Performance Superelastic Materials for Seismic Applications

Buildings that survive an earthquake can still be lost if they are left leaning. My thesis asks whether an ultra-strong shape memory alloy, NiTiHfPd, can pull steel buildings back to plumb after shaking, and turns the answer into a new device: the SMA re-centering damper (SMARD).

Chapter 1

An alloy that remembers its shape

Superelastic shape memory alloys can be squeezed several percent and spring back with almost no permanent deformation. Their stress–strain curve is a flag: a plateau on loading, a lower plateau on unloading, and a return to zero.

Ordinary NiTi is limited in strength and changes behavior with temperature. I tested a new alloy, Ni45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5, in compression at −35, 5 and 25 °C and at 0.05, 0.5 and 1 Hz, then built a numerical model of its response for structural analysis.

  • Specimen4 × 4 × 8 mm, heat treated at 400 °C for 3 h
  • Test machine100 kN MTS servo-hydraulic frame, 12 mm high-temperature extensometer
  • Modeltwo flag-shaped elements in parallel, fitted to the 1 Hz tests (Table 3 of the paper)

Chapter 2

SMA braces bring a 4-story frame back to plumb

A steel moment frame can survive a strong earthquake but stay permanently tilted, and a building left leaning more than about 0.5% is often demolished.

I designed a 4-story frame with NiTiHfPd braces in a chevron layout in every bay and story, with a lighter frame so that strength and stiffness match the original. Both frames were analyzed under 20 SAC ground motions for Los Angeles at three hazard levels: 50%, 10% and 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years.

The braced frame matched the original in peak drift and floor acceleration but cut the drift left behind dramatically, and its performance held up from −35 to 25 °C.

Chapter 3

The SMA re-centering damper (SMARD)

The SMARD combines two jobs in one compact device: NiTiHfPd bars that re-center the structure, and friction springs that dissipate energy and add stroke.

A piston runs through a housing with baffles. Whichever way it moves, it pushes one plate while the baffle holds the other, so the bars and springs are always compressed. The result is a flag-shaped force–displacement loop.

  • Capacity3600 kN from four 900 kN friction-spring groups; 150 mm stroke; loss factor over 0.30
  • SMA bars25 mm diameter, 200 mm long, design stress 1800 MPa
  • Buildings6-story and 9-story steel moment frames in Seattle, two SMARDs per story in chevron braces
  • AnalysisOpenSees, 44 far-field records, design (DBE) and maximum (MCE) earthquakes

Conclusions

What the thesis shows

A strong, damping alloyNiTiHfPd keeps its superelastic flag from −35 to 25 °C; colder means more damping, and from 5 to 25 °C the change is under 10%.
Braces that re-centerIn a 4-story frame, SMA braces cut the median residual drift at the rarest shaking from 4.31% to 0.69%, with similar peak drifts and accelerations.
A practical deviceThe SMARD sharply reduced drifts, residual drifts and collapses in 6- and 9-story frames, with only a small increase in floor acceleration.
