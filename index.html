Teaching philosophy

Students rarely fail to learn mathematics because they lack the capacity for it; they disengage from it because they cannot see why it matters to them.

My goal in every course I teach is to close that gap: to make the mathematics rigorous, but also visibly and repeatedly relevant to the discipline each student came to study. My own research on engineering students at UVA sharpened this view. Our data showed that many capable students carry real anxiety about their mathematical ability, and that it shapes their academic choices. Confidence-building is not a soft add-on to rigorous instruction; it is a prerequisite for it.

Mathematics in context

I frame each topic around the problems students will use it to solve: modeling dynamic systems with differential equations, analyzing uncertainty with probability and statistics, and applying linear algebra to structural and computational problems.

Active, not passive

Trained through UVA's Center for Teaching Excellence, I replace passive lecture time with structured problem-solving and inquiry-based activities, so students discover ideas while help is in the room.

Low stakes first

Early assessments are diagnostic and low-stakes, so misconceptions surface before they compound and students see where they stand.

Office hours without embarrassment

I make office hours a low-pressure space to ask foundational questions, because the students who most need help are often the least comfortable asking for it.

How I teach: two evidence-based methods

Ordinary Differential Equations

Inquiry-based (discovery) learning

In ODE I use an inquiry-based approach in which students discover the material themselves: they work through carefully sequenced problems, make conjectures and build the methods before the formal treatment. The approach comes from an NSF-funded project and has been used in UVA's Applied Mathematics Center for the last six years. It has proved highly effective, and students really like it.

Calculus I · Probability

Mastery-based grading

In Calculus I and Probability I used a mastery grading system, in which students show mastery of each core skill and grades reflect what they have learned. It was effective for most of the class. My co-authored ASEE 2024 study, Exploring the Impact of Mastery Grading on Student Performance, examines this approach.

Mathematics education for engineering students

With colleagues at UVA I published three studies at the ASEE Annual Conference. The first examined how remote instruction during COVID-19 affected students in applied mathematics courses for engineers. The second used a grading option to measure engineering students' confidence in mathematics. The third explored how mastery grading affects student performance. These findings shape how I structure my own courses.

Shape memory alloy dampers

My M.Sc. thesis at UVA, advised by Prof. O. E. Ozbulut, developed self-centering control devices that use shape memory alloys to reduce seismic response at several hazard levels. A related paper in Smart Materials and Structures (2022) studies steel frames upgraded with NiTiHfPd re-centering dampers.

Read the paper · Smart Materials and Structures SPIE 2022 paper (PDF)

Self-centering SMA braces

With F. Shi, G. P. Toker, O. E. Ozbulut and H. E. Karaca, I studied NiTi-based shape memory alloy braces for steel buildings (11th U.S. National Conference on Earthquake Engineering, 2018). The braces sit in a chevron layout in every bay of every story of a 4-story steel frame and work only in tension: as the building sways, one brace of each pair stretches and the other goes slack. Their superelastic, flag-shaped response dissipates energy and then pulls the building back to plumb. Under the strongest ground motions, the median residual drift fell from 4.31% for the moment frame to 0.69% with SMA braces, and the performance stayed nearly the same from −35 °C to 25 °C.

Read the paper (PDF) · 11NCEE 2018

Optimization with graph theory

My first M.Sc. thesis, advised by Prof. Ali Kaveh, proposed seven graph-theoretical algorithms for improving the conditioning of structural flexibility matrices. Five of them appear in Engineering Computations (2022). The algorithms choose which cycles of a frame's graph model are used in the force method. Short cycles make the flexibility matrix sparse; cycles chosen by member weight make it better conditioned, so round-off errors shrink. Sparsity and conditioning cannot both be optimized at once, so the problem is a multi-objective one.

Read the paper · Engineering Computations

Neural networks for hysteresis

With M. Farrokh and M. Shafiei Dizaji, I developed the Extended Preisach Neural Network (EPNN), published in Neural Processing Letters (2022). Ordinary neural networks lack the memory needed to reproduce hysteresis, where the output depends on the path the input has taken. The EPNN has two hidden layers. Its deteriorating stop (DS) neurons each act like a spring pulling a block across a friction surface, which gives the network memory and lets loops degrade; its sigmoid neurons shape non-Masing and asymmetric loops; and an extra input for the rate ẋ(t) lets it learn rate-dependent hysteresis. Trained with a hybrid of a genetic algorithm and sub-gradient optimization, it agreed well with experimental hysteresis from ferromagnetic materials, magnetostrictive actuators and shape memory alloy actuators.

Read the paper · Neural Processing Letters
